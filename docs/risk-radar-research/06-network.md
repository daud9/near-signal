# Finality, block production, and shard congestion risk

## Verified RPC surfaces and definitions

NEAR’s `block` JSON-RPC method accepts exactly one of `block_id` or `finality`; the documented finality values are `optimistic`, `near-final`, and `final`. A response contains `header.height`, `header.hash`, `header.timestamp`/`timestamp_nanosec`, `header.prev_hash`, `header.last_final_block`, `header.gas_price`, and a `chunks` array. Each chunk header includes `shard_id`, `gas_used`, `gas_limit`, `height_created`, `height_included`, and, on current protocol versions, nullable `congestion_info` with `allowed_shard`, `buffered_receipts_gas`, `delayed_receipts_gas`, and `receipt_bytes`. [1] [2]

The `status` method describes the connected RPC node, not an independent network oracle. Its response includes `sync_info` (`latest_block_height`, `latest_block_hash`, `latest_block_time`, `earliest_block_height`, `syncing`, `epoch_id`, and related fields), `version.version`, active `protocol_version`, `latest_protocol_version`, and a node-local `validators` list. `syncing:false` means that this node reports itself synced; it does not prove that every RPC provider, validator, shard, or client is healthy. [3]

`validators` is a separate method. It can be queried by `latest`, `epoch_id`, or `block_id`, and returns `epoch_height`, `epoch_start_height`, `current_validators`, `next_validators`, and `prev_epoch_kickout`. It is suitable for observing validator-set and kickout changes, but not for proving that a particular producer made every expected block. [4]

NEAR exposes `EXPERIMENTAL_congestion_level`, taking either `{block_id, shard_id}` or `{chunk_id}` and returning a numeric `congestion_level`. The API reference does not define a universal alert cutoff in the endpoint schema. Its interpretation must therefore be version-aware and calibrated from repeated samples. [5] The nearcore receipt-congestion design explains the underlying model: flow is measured in gas, shard sink capacity is modeled as 1000 Tgas, delayed receipts are a queue/backpressure mechanism, and the design intentionally applies graduated limits rather than a single binary state. [6]

FastNEAR’s free NEARDATA service provides `/v0/last_block/final`, `/v0/last_block/optimistic`, `/v0/block/:height`, and smaller `/headers`, `/chunk/:shard_id`, and `/shard/:shard_id` routes. The finalized route is a useful second observation stream for finalized block height/hash and per-shard chunk data. FastNEAR documents a 180 requests/minute/IP limit and says that the service is unauthenticated. [7]

## Live test evidence captured

A live query was made against `https://rpc.mainnet.fastnear.com` and `https://mainnet.neardata.xyz/v0/last_block/final` during this research. The FastNEAR RPC `status` result reported `chain_id: mainnet`, `protocol_version: 88`, `version.version: 2.14.1`, `sync_info.syncing: false`, and `latest_block_height: 224557645`. The same RPC returned a finalized block with `header.height: 224557623`, so the node’s latest optimistic/sync height was 22 blocks above the queried finalized block at capture time. The finalized block contained 10 chunks. Every sampled chunk had `gas_limit: 1000000000000000` (1000 Tgas); the largest observed `gas_used` was about 43.92 Tgas, or 4.39% of that chunk limit. Every sampled `congestion_info.delayed_receipts_gas`, `buffered_receipts_gas`, and `receipt_bytes` was zero. NEARDATA independently returned a later finalized block (`header.height: 224557683`) and also exposed the same chunk-level fields. These are observed facts from one capture, not a claim of sustained health.

**Observed test result:** no finality-stall, node-sync, gas-utilization, or delayed/buffered-receipt alert fired in this single capture. This is not evidence that the risks are absent. A real alert requires a time series and, for provider-independent claims, a second provider or NEARDATA comparison.

## Candidate live signals and implementation rules

1. **Finalized-height progress / stall.** Poll `block {finality:"final"}` from FastNEAR RPC and `/v0/last_block/final` from NEARDATA every 5–10 seconds. Record `(height, hash, timestamp_nanosec)` from each response. For each source, compute `delta_height = h_t - h_{t-W}` and wall-clock age `now - timestamp`. A defensible **critical** rule is `delta_height = 0` for at least 30 seconds while the other source advances by at least 5 blocks; this is a provider/path failure candidate, not proof of chain finality failure. A **high** network-finality rule requires two independent sources to show no finalized-height increase for 30 seconds and then checks the next window; avoid paging on one missed response. Evidence is the raw sequence of heights, hashes, timestamps, HTTP/RPC errors, and provider identity. This can run now with FastNEAR RPC plus existing FastNEAR NEARDATA.

2. **Finality lag versus optimistic production.** Query `block(finality:"optimistic")` and `block(finality:"final")` from the same provider and compute `L_h = optimistic_height - finalized_height`. Also compute `L_s` from header timestamps, converting nanoseconds to seconds. Do not treat the ordinary small lag as risk without a baseline. A practical tested-style rule is: warning when `L_h` is above the rolling 99th percentile or above 30 blocks for 3 consecutive polls; critical when it is above 60 blocks for 3 polls **and** a second source reports the same direction. The numbers are operational guardrails, not protocol guarantees; calibrate them from at least 24 hours of one-second-ish samples. The evidence must include both block headers and the sample window. This can run now with the supplied RPC and NEARDATA finalized feed.

3. **Block-production cadence and missing heights.** From finalized headers, compute `dt_i = (timestamp_i - timestamp_{i-1}) / 1e9` and `dh_i = height_i - height_{i-1}`. For a contiguous sequence, a rough block interval is `dt_i/dh_i`; retain skipped-height evidence rather than silently interpolating. Because NEAR’s gas documentation describes approximately one-second block production, a conservative operational rule is warning if the median interval over 15 consecutive finalized blocks exceeds 3 seconds, and critical if it exceeds 10 seconds for 3 consecutive windows or if both sources stop advancing. These are service-level thresholds derived from the documented approximate cadence, not consensus limits. Evidence is the ordered `(height, timestamp, hash, prev_hash)` sequence. This can run now with NEARDATA headers or RPC blocks.

4. **Per-shard gas utilization.** For every finalized `block.chunks[]`, compute `u_s = gas_used / gas_limit` per `shard_id`; aggregate `max_s(u_s)` and the count of shards over a threshold. The documented chunk cap is 1000 Tgas, and the gas docs say a chunk can burn at most 1000 Tgas. A useful **warning** heuristic is `u_s >= 0.80` for the same shard in 5 of 10 consecutive blocks; **high** is `u_s >= 0.95` in 5 of 10; **critical** should require `u_s >= 0.99` in 3 consecutive blocks plus increasing delayed receipts or transaction execution delay. Do not call 80% a protocol-defined danger line: it is a capacity headroom rule. Evidence is the per-height, per-shard numerator/denominator and block hashes. This can run now from FastNEAR `block` or NEARDATA finalized block/chunk routes.

5. **Per-shard delayed/buffered receipt pressure.** Read `chunks[].congestion_info.delayed_receipts_gas`, `buffered_receipts_gas`, and `receipt_bytes`. Normalize gas values to Tgas by dividing the integer gas units by `1e12`, and retain the raw integer. Alert on trend, not one nonzero value: warning when delayed or buffered gas is positive and increasing in at least 5 of 10 consecutive observations for one shard; high when it is positive in 10 of 10 and the 10-sample slope is positive; critical when it remains positive while the shard’s `gas_used/gas_limit` is >=0.80 and finalized block progress or transaction completion latency worsens. The nearcore design supports queue/backpressure interpretation but does not publish a universal numeric threshold, so these are operational rules requiring calibration. Evidence is the full per-shard series and the associated chunk headers. This can run now with current FastNEAR RPC/NEARDATA fields when `congestion_info` is present.

6. **Experimental congestion level.** Call `EXPERIMENTAL_congestion_level` with `{block_id: finalized_height, shard_id}` or the chunk hash, and store the returned `congestion_level`. Run it for every shard over at least 30–60 observations. Use warning at the shard’s rolling 95th percentile for 5/10 samples and high at the rolling 99th percentile for 5/10; only use an absolute cutoff after confirming the provider’s nearcore version and semantics. A single response cannot establish risk because the endpoint is explicitly experimental and provider-local. The official free RPC method can be added without a paid API, but support should be tested on the chosen FastNEAR endpoint before depending on it. Evidence is request block/chunk identity, shard ID, returned level, provider, and version.

7. **Gas-price demand corroboration.** Call `gas_price [null]` or use `header.gas_price`; the response field is `result.gas_price` in yoctoNEAR. NEAR documents that gas price is recalculated each block, increases 1% when the previous block is more than half full, and floors at 1 Tgas = 0.0001 NEAR. Compute the percentage change over 30–60 blocks and compare it with per-shard utilization and receipt pressure. A warning can be a sustained positive gas-price slope for 30 blocks together with at least one shard’s high-utilization rule; gas price alone is not a shard-congestion alert because the documented mechanism is network-wide/demand-based in the current gas documentation. This can run now from the supplied RPC.

8. **Provider divergence / source-confidence signal.** Compare finalized `(height, hash)` from FastNEAR RPC and NEARDATA. If heights differ by more than 5 blocks or hashes disagree for the same height, record a source-confidence warning and fetch a third free public RPC such as an official NEAR endpoint for adjudication. Do not convert this directly into network-impact risk: divergence can be stale cache, routing, archival lag, or an RPC fault. This separation is essential when only one provider is available.

## What one block or one provider cannot prove

One RPC response cannot establish a sustained finality delay, a missed-producer rate, a chain halt, or a global congestion event. A block’s `author` identifies the block author, but the response does not by itself prove that all scheduled producers produced on time. `gas_used/gas_limit` measures execution gas in that chunk, not user-perceived transaction latency, receipt queue age, bandwidth saturation, or future demand. `congestion_info` is a snapshot associated with the returned chunk; a zero queue in one block does not rule out a queue in the next block.

A provider’s `status.syncing:false` is node-local. Its validator list is a view returned by that node and should be compared by epoch/block identity before interpreting changes. A provider may omit or reject experimental methods, may lag, cache, route to a partial node, or have a clock issue. A disagreement between FastNEAR RPC and NEARDATA is first a source-confidence event; it becomes a network-impact event only after independent sources converge and the discrepancy persists.

The currently connected feeds do not provide a dependable public, historical, per-transaction finality-latency dataset or a guaranteed cross-provider RPC quorum. Those require storing repeated observations locally or adding a free public RPC source. CoinGecko and DefiLlama prices/TVL are not evidence of block production or shard congestion and should not be used to manufacture a network alert.
